04 — Principles

How I build software.

Applied where they earn their keep — not as dogma that adds complexity nobody asked for.

Separation of concerns

Business rules live in services. No pricing logic in a controller, no queries in a template.

Strongly typed

C# and TypeScript types as documentation and guardrails. Catch it at compile time, not in support.

Fail fast, fail cheap

Validate before the expensive call. Invalid input stops at the edge, not three services deep.

Query-conscious

Every grid has a query plan behind it. Reporting screens get indexes, not hope.

Business logic first

I learn how your pricing, inventory or itinerary rules actually work before I model them.

Configurable, not hard-coded

Rules the business can change themselves beat a ticket to me every time a rate moves.

Proactive updates

You hear about a blocker the day it appears, with options attached. No silent weeks.

Small, reviewable changes

Short-lived branches and readable commits, so a teammate can pick up where I left off.

Automate the repetitive

Recurring jobs and scheduled syncs instead of somebody remembering to press a button.

Observable by default

Job dashboards, sync history and execution logs ship with the feature, not after the first incident.

Boring releases

CI/CD pipelines, migrations in version control, a rollback path. Deploys should be uneventful.

Around after go-live

Handover notes, and I stay reachable for the questions that only appear in production.

07 — FAQ

Questions I usually get.

Anything not answered here, just ask — I reply within a working day.

Which time zone do you work in? +

I am in Ahmedabad, India (UTC+5:30) and work a substantial overlap with UK and European hours — I have been delivering for a UK client since 2022, so calls, reviews and releases already run on that rhythm.

What work is the best fit for you? +

Business systems where the rules are the hard part: pricing and promotion engines, multichannel e-commerce automation, inventory and warehouse operations, reporting over large datasets. Stack-wise that is .NET, C#, ASP.NET Core, Angular and SQL Server, with AWS around it.

Can you join an existing team? +

Yes — most of my work is exactly that. I work inside your repository, your branching model and your board, join standups and reviews, and follow the conventions already in the codebase rather than importing my own.

What do your rates look like? +

They depend on scope, duration and how much notice you need — a rolling monthly retainer is priced differently from a one-week rescue. Send the brief by email and you will get a concrete number, not a range.

How do you start an engagement? +

A short call to understand the domain, then a written summary of scope, assumptions and sequence. If you would rather test the working relationship first, a small paid task is a good way to do it before anything longer is signed.

What happens at handover? +

Migrations and configuration live in version control, jobs and syncs come with dashboards and logs, and you get concise notes plus a walkthrough — the essentials your team needs, not a document nobody opens. I stay reachable afterwards for the questions that only appear in production.
